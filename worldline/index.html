worldline

A spacetime diagram puts time up the page and space across it. Every dot is an event: something that happens at one place at one moment. Light always travels along the 45° lines. Change who is looking and the dots slide along hyperbolas, but light stays at 45°, and which events count as "at the same time" tilts with the observer.

    Reading the diagram

    The blue grid belongs to the lab. Its vertical lines are clocks bolted to the lab floor, one light-second apart; its horizontal lines are the lab's moments. The orange grid belongs to a rocket. A clock riding in the rocket traces a tilted line, and the rocket's moments, the events it calls simultaneous, tilt by the same angle toward the light line. That second tilt is the relativity of simultaneity.

    The interval, time apart squared minus distance apart squared, comes out the same for every observer. When it is positive the events are timelike: a clock can be at both, and everyone agrees which came first. When it is negative they are spacelike: no signal joins them, and observers moving fast enough in opposite directions disagree on their order. Pick two events and slide "Look from" to watch it happen.

    The scenes are the standard puzzles of the subject. In each one the numbers in the account come from the same formulas that draw the picture, so moving the observer changes how the scene looks and leaves every interval and every clock reading where it was.
