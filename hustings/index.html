Reading the map

Every voter likes candidates more the nearer they stand, and ranks them by distance. A point of the map stands for a whole electorate: a round cloud of voters centred there, the same cloud each time. The slider sets how far the cloud spreads.

If the cloud shrank to a single point, every voter would cast the same ballot and every rule would give the same map: each candidate would win the points nearer to them than to anyone else. The rules differ once the cloud spreads and voters' second and third choices start to count.

Plurality hears only first choices. Two candidates who stand close together split their supporters, and a third candidate wins ground that neither would lose alone. In the Twins scene the far candidate's region bulges into the gap where the cloud is split between the twins.

A Condorcet winner beats every rival head to head. Every rule here except minimax can elect someone else. In the Centre squeeze scene a moderate everyone prefers to the extremes collects too few first choices to survive the first round of a runoff.

Instant runoff gives B two thin stripes in the Centre squeeze, one on each side of the middle, and loses B the middle itself, where B stands. In a stripe the far candidate has the fewest first choices and drops out first, and those ballots carry B past the near one. In the middle B has the fewest and drops out.

Minimax always elects the Condorcet winner when there is one. With voters ranked by distance in a round cloud there almost always is one, so its map is the reference the stripes compare against.

These pictures are known as Yee diagrams, after Ka-Ping Yee, who drew them in 2006 to compare voting rules. Approval here assumes each voter approves every candidate nearer than their average distance; other habits give other maps. Under this habit a candidate standing exactly halfway between two others is approved by every voter, because their distance to that candidate is never more than the average of their distances to the other two. That is why approval hands B the whole Centre squeeze map.
