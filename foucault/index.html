foucault

A heavy bob on a long wire keeps swinging in the same plane while the floor turns beneath it. Seen from the floor the swing slowly turns: once a day at the poles, never at the equator, and once every 31.8 hours in Paris, where Foucault first showed it in 1851.

Why sin φ

Over a day the Earth carries the pendulum once around its parallel. Take the cone that touches the globe all along that parallel: it can be cut and laid flat without stretching, and on the flat sheet a swing that is never twisted keeps one direction (gold), while north (blue) always points along the cone to its tip. Laid flat, the cone is a sector of 360° × sin φ, so by the time the pendulum is back where it started, north has turned that far past the swing.

Why the wire is long

A real swing is never quite flat. Let the bob go with the slightest sideways slip and it traces a thin ellipse, and a wide ellipse on a spherical pendulum turns by itself, in the sense it goes round, at Airy's rate (3/8) ω0 a b / L². On a short wire that can outrun the Earth. This runs the full pendulum, wide swings included, for two hours at the latitude chosen above.
