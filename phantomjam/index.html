Click a car on the ring to make it brake. Sweep density walks the density from 0.02 to 0.98, holding each value long enough to measure the flow, and fills in the diagram. Shortcuts: Space pause, . step, B brake a car, R reset, D sweep, [/] previous or next regime.

This is the Nagel–Schreckenberg model (1992). Every step, each car speeds up by one, slows to the gap in front, and with the dawdling probability drops one notch for no reason; then everyone moves at once. That single random hesitation is enough: below a critical density it is absorbed, above it the follower brakes harder than the leader did and a jam is born, drifting upstream while the cars that caused it drive off. Flow peaks at the critical density, so adding cars to a busy road carries fewer of them past any point.
